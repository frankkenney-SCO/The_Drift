One law, three business models: a digital promise drifts from physical or financial reality, and the gap is the leak.

Every business model here carries Margin Drift. Each one also carries Utilization Drift — the same shape, applied to whatever's actually scarce for them: truck-hours, warehouse slots, or working capital.

Margin Drift

The master arc
WhoWarehouses and 3PLs — the same order-economics erosion, billed per unit or per pallet instead of per mile.
ModelPriced on the perfect order: quoted rate, planned dwell, no accessorials. Real orders erode that margin at every stage — extra dwell, damage claims, storage overages — and it only ever subtracts, so it compounds linearly toward settlement.
CurrencyDollars of margin — quoted rate vs. realized rate.

Warehouse Utilization Drift

Same shape as the truck, different asset
WhoWarehouses and 3PLs — the building's capacity, not a single order.
ModelPriced on the assumption that storage slots and dock doors are continuously occupied and earning. Each turnover cycle opens a gap and closes it again once new inventory arrives — cyclical, not compounding, same shape as a truck's utilization cycle, just measured in square footage instead of miles.
CurrencySlot and dock-hours — storage capacity that held no inventory or produced no throughput.

Margin & Utilization Drift

Same second axis, cash instead of hours
WhoBrokerages — margin risk lands at Commitment, utilization risk lands at Settlement. Two different mechanisms, same order.
ModelThe diamond at Commitment is guidance, not drift — a CRO who tells investors "margin between A% and B%" is stating that range on purpose, and landing anywhere inside it is the plan working as intended. Real Margin Drift only starts the moment the actual spread breaks through the top or bottom edge of that band. Status is just an operational checkpoint against carrier data to trigger close-out, not a new source of drift. The second drift opens once the carrier is paid: cash goes out, sits idle while the shipper hasn't paid yet, then recovers when they finally do. Same open-hold-recover cycle a truck runs between loads or a storage slot runs between turnovers — just working capital instead of a truck or a slot.
CurrencyTwo currencies in one chart — dollars of margin at Commitment, cash-days of idle working capital at Settlement.

The real signal: blended margin over time

The diamond is per-load variance, and it's assumed — closing above or below the guided range on any single quote is the model working as intended. Real Margin Drift isn't a point in the diamond. It's the blended average, across a week or a month of loads, drifting away from target for long enough that it stops being noise.

Margin Drift

The master arc
WhoAsset-based carriers — companies that own the truck and sell capacity directly.
ModelPriced on the perfect order: quoted rate, planned lane, full margin as quoted. Every real-world stage can only subtract from that plan, never add back — so the drift compounds roughly linearly the further down the process you go. Detention sits inside this, but precisely: the delay itself isn't the loss, since a fully-paid detention claim is revenue-neutral. The loss is the collection gap — billed detention minus what's actually approved and paid at invoice reconciliation, before the invoice is ever accepted.
CurrencyDollars of margin — quoted rate vs. realized rate.

Utilization Drift

Zooms into Status
WhoThe same asset-based carriers — but about the truck, not the order.
ModelPriced on the assumption the truck earns during every available hour. Each load cycle opens a utilization gap and closes it again once the truck is loaded — cyclical, not compounding.
CurrencyTruck-hours — capacity that produced no revenue.
